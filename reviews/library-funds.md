# Library Funds

## [Library Funds](https://library.gsu.edu/giving/library-funds/)

Below you will find all the funds that are available to support. To donate:

- visit the [Georgia State University Foundation giving site](https://donate.gsu.edu/s/give?appeal=NET_RED "Original URL: https://donate.gsu.edu/s/give?appeal=NET_RED. Click or tap if you trust this link."),
- click on “Select More+ under “Designations,” and
- search by fund name or fund code.

**Endowments –** Many individuals choose to give an enduring gift to Georgia State by creating an endowed fund. After reaching $25,000, the principal of the endowment is invested. About 4% is rolled off every year into the spendable fund and used in accordance with the purpose of the fund. Gifts can be made to both the principal to grow the endowment and to the spendable fund to address immediate needs.

**Funds –** Funds are non-endowed but still fulfill specific needs of the Library. The entire amount of a fund is available for use in accordance with the purpose of the fund.

#### General Programmatic Funds

**GSU Fund for the University Library – 02D49**

Meets the pressing needs of students for access to current technology, great learning spaces, professional expertise and rich information resources.

**Library Future Fund – 02C97**

Contribute to improvements of our dynamic, student-centered space that inspires learning and facilitates connections. The Georgia State University Library, as it seeks to be a national model of the innovative research library, aims to provide dynamic physical and virtual spaces in support of our Strategic Intentions. With this in mind, the University Library has developed a master plan for the Atlanta campus library. The result is a plan which will guide our space decisions in the years ahead.

**Library Research Award Fund – 020061**

Offered annually to doctoral students in any discipline to help fund travel to a museum, archive or library to complete dissertation research. It also allows for prize money to go to 3 Minute Thesis winners, presented by the Library and Graduate Programs.

**Perimeter College Libraries Excellence Fund – 021605**

Provides funding for special projects at the five Perimeter College library locations.

#### General Programmatic Endowments

**Carolyn L. Robison Library Award – 02A99**

The purpose of the fund is to provide professional development funding for Library staff members who represent educational values and high professional standards.

**Anne Page Mosby Endowment – 020028**

The spendable portion of the fund will be available to the Dean of Libraries to support the professional development and creative activities of the GSU Library faculty and staff. Funds may be used for such expenses as research, presentations at conferences, workshops and/or professional publishing costs. Funds may also be used for travel and other expenses related to faculty and staff members participating in or attending conference, workshops, retreats and other development activities aimed at developing library employees.

**Arthur Vandenberg Library Innovation Fund – 020285**

The purpose of the fund is to support and provide for innovative technology-based learning or research opportunities within the Georgia State University Library.

**Dorothy Stamps Ingram Fund for Library Outreach – 020294**

The purpose of the fund is to help position the Georgia State University Library as a top research library and community resource by providing support to promote community outreach.

**Jane G. Hobson Endowment for the University Library — 021397**

In honor of Jane G. Hobson, a long-time serving faculty member of the University LIbrary. Every year, the revenue will be used at the Dean’s discretion, with priority given to projects that honor Jane’s work and passions as a librarian.

**Wayne Daniel Endowment – 02E36**

Provides funding to collect, preserve, and make available to researchers, materials related to country music, bluegrass music, and Southern gospel music.  Funds may also be used for a travel award of at least $500 to be given annually to a graduate student or early career professional (3-5 years from completion of terminal degree) whose research utilizing the [Wayne W.
